### How I got here

I started in actuarial science because I liked the idea of putting an honest number on uncertainty. Somewhere along the way, "how much should this cost" turned into "why do people price risk the way they actually do," and that question is what keeps me building. My current research, recovering how investors trade off risk and return from JSE sector data, is really me trying to answer it with data instead of intuition.

I care more about finishing the whole pipeline 
<p align="center">data &#8594; model &#8594; decision &#8594; something a non-technical person can actually use </p>
than about a clever result stuck in a notebook. If it can't survive contact with a real user or a real out-of-sample period, it doesn't count yet.

### What I've built

**📈 Statistical Arbitrage Engine**
Started as "can I make a systematic signal actually hold up out-of-sample." Became a full MS-GARCH regime-detection and Bivariate Vine Copula pairs framework trading Standard Bank against Nedbank: 6.2% CAGR, 0.90 Sharpe, walk-forward validated across 503 trades.

**🏥 [Health Insurance Pricing Model](https://github.com/Sbusiso-Mdingi/health-insurance-pricing-model)**
A frequency–severity pricing engine in R, deployed as a Shiny dashboard so someone with zero actuarial background can move a slider and watch their premium respond. Built to mirror how insurers actually price, not a toy example.

**🔐 [Secure Steganographic Messaging System](https://github.com/Sbusiso-Mdingi/stego-messenger)**
The odd one out on this profile: cryptography and image steganography, built with the threat modelling and adversarial testing you'd expect from a real security product. Turns out "quant" and "security" scratch the same itch: both are about finding what's hidden in the noise.

**📊 [Cross-Line Tail Risk Modelling](https://github.com/Sbusiso-Mdingi/cross-line-tail-risk-modelling)**
Copula-based tail dependence between life insurance mortality losses and property catastrophe claims, because diversification benefits tend to vanish exactly when you need them most, and I wanted to know by how much.
